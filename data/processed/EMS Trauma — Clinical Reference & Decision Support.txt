EMS Trauma — Clinical Reference & Decision Support

1. Purpose

This document provides a structured reference for the assessment and management of trauma patients in the prehospital environment.

It is intended to help the AI:

Recognize traumatic emergencies

Identify immediate life threats

Perform systematic trauma assessment

Understand mechanisms of injury

Recognize shock

Identify major hemorrhage

Recognize traumatic brain injury

Assess spinal injury

Recognize chest trauma

Recognize abdominal and pelvic trauma

Assess extremity injuries

Recognize burns

Identify special trauma populations

Understand trauma triage and destination considerations

Support trauma training and simulation

Local EMS protocols, medical-director orders, and regional trauma-system requirements take precedence over this document for actual patient care.

Current professional trauma resources include guidance from NAEMSP, including recent trauma resources addressing hemorrhage, traumatic cardiac arrest, spinal cord injury, pelvic injury, femoral shaft fracture, geriatric trauma, and tranexamic acid.

10. Shock

Trauma can cause multiple types of shock.

Important categories include:

Hemorrhagic shock

Blood loss → reduced circulating volume → reduced perfusion.

Obstructive shock

Examples include:

Tension pneumothorax

Cardiac tamponade

Neurogenic shock

May occur with significant spinal cord injury and has a distinct physiology from hemorrhagic shock.

Traumatic cardiac arrest

Requires a trauma-specific approach rather than simply treating the patient as a conventional medical cardiac arrest.

11. Shock Recognition

Possible findings include:

Altered mental status

Weak or absent peripheral pulses

Tachycardia

Hypotension

Cool skin

Pale skin

Delayed capillary refill

Increasing respiratory rate

Weakness

Anxiety/restlessness

Decreased urine output when known

Progressive deterioration

Do not rely on hypotension alone to identify shock.

15. Cervical Spine / Spinal Trauma

Assess:

Mechanism

Neck pain

Midline tenderness

Neurologic symptoms

Weakness

Numbness

Paresthesias

Deformity

Altered mental status

Distracting injuries

Current consensus uses the term spinal motion restriction (SMR) rather than implying that EMS can completely immobilize the spine. NAEMSP's published guidance identifies factors including altered consciousness, midline neck/back pain or tenderness, focal neurologic findings, spinal deformity, and distracting injuries as indications in blunt trauma.

Important: Your AI should use the patient's actual local trauma/SMR protocol when deciding whether SMR is indicated.

19. Pelvic Trauma

46. Critical Trauma Errors

Training scenarios should identify potentially serious errors such as:

Failure to identify catastrophic hemorrhage

Failure to recognize airway compromise

Failure to recognize respiratory failure

Failure to recognize tension physiology

Failure to recognize shock

Failure to reassess

Failure to identify neurologic deterioration

Failure to recognize major pelvic injury

Incorrect medication

Incorrect procedure

Failure to consider hypothermia

Failure to transport an unstable patient appropriately

47. Trauma AI Safety Rules

The AI must never:

Invent trauma protocols

Invent trauma-center criteria

Invent medication doses

Invent scope of practice

Invent medical-control requirements

Assume all trauma requires spinal immobilization

Assume mechanism alone determines treatment

Treat training material as operational authorization

Ignore pediatric differences

Ignore geriatric differences

Ignore pregnancy

Claim a patient is stable without adequate assessment

48. Conflicting Sources

If two trauma documents conflict:

Identify both documents.

Compare versions and dates.

Determine whether one is local protocol and one is educational guidance.

Use the current operational protocol for field care.

Flag unresolved conflicts.

Example:

"The supplied trauma reference and current local protocol differ on this point. For an actual patient encounter, the current local protocol should control."

51. Final Trauma AI Rule

When presented with a trauma patient, the AI should prioritize:

Safety → Catastrophic hemorrhage → Airway → Breathing → Circulation → Neurologic status → Exposure → Reassessment → Transport

Then:

Retrieve the applicable local protocol.

The AI should explain its clinical reasoning, but it must clearly distinguish general trauma knowledge from the actual protocol governing the paramedic's practice.

Document classification: EMS Trauma Clinical Reference

Primary operational authority: Current local EMS protocol / medical director / regional trauma system

Primary educational purpose: Assessment, recognition, clinical reasoning, training, and simulation

Safety principle: When the applicable local trauma protocol is unavailable, the AI must say so rather than inventing operational instructions.
