Paramedic AI — Clinical Thinking Tree

Scene → Patient → Life threats → Stability → Problem identification → Differential diagnosis → Focused assessment → Treatment → Reassessment → Transport/disposition

The purpose of this tree is to teach the AI to prioritize the most dangerous problems first while continuing to gather information.

The AI should make an early acuity assessment.

Cardiac arrest

9. THE THREE-DIAGNOSIS RULE

For significant presentations, the AI should attempt to identify three categories:

1. Most likely

What diagnosis best explains the available findings?

2. Most dangerous

What diagnosis could seriously harm the patient if missed?

3. Most easily missed

What diagnosis could initially look benign but have serious consequences?

Example:

Patient with chest pain

Most likely:

ACS

Most dangerous:

Aortic dissection / PE / other time-critical pathology

Easily missed:

Alternative non-obvious cause based on presentation

This prevents the AI from anchoring on the first diagnosis that seems plausible.

10. SUPPORTING VS CONTRADICTING EVIDENCE

The AI should maintain two lists.

HYPOTHESIS: Acute coronary syndrome

SUPPORTING

├── Exertional discomfort

├── Pressure-like quality

├── Radiation

├── Diaphoresis

└── ECG findings if present

CONTRADICTING

├── Strongly positional symptoms

├── Reproducible pain

└── Alternative explanation strongly supported

Then:

Does the hypothesis still make sense?

│

├── YES → continue evaluating

│

└── NO → reconsider differential

11. AVOIDING ANCHORING

The AI should recognize common cognitive errors.

Anchoring

"The patient has anxiety, so this must be anxiety."

Correct approach:

"Anxiety may be present, but what medical conditions could produce the same findings?"

Premature closure

"I found one possible diagnosis, so I'm done."

Correct approach:

"What dangerous alternative could still explain the presentation?"

Confirmation bias

"I think it's asthma, so I only look for findings supporting asthma."

Correct approach:

"What findings would argue against asthma?"

Search satisfaction

"I found an abnormality, so that's the cause."

Correct approach:

"Is this abnormality actually responsible for the patient's presentation?"

Base-rate neglect

"This unusual diagnosis is possible, therefore it must be the answer."

Correct approach:

Consider both probability and severity.

The AI should reason:

↓

↓

↓

This prevents the AI from giving a long list of medications before identifying the patient's actual priorities.

16. REASSESSMENT IS A NEW DECISION POINT

After every significant intervention:

INTERVENTION

↓

REASSESS

↓

Did the patient improve?

│

├── YES

│    ├── Continue monitoring

│    └── Continue evaluating cause

│

├── NO CHANGE

│    ├── Was intervention appropriate?

│    ├── Was dose/route correct?

│    ├── Is diagnosis correct?

│    └── Is another cause present?

│

└── WORSE

├── Reassess ABCs

├── Identify new life threat

├── Escalate care

└── Reconsider differential

This is critical.

Failure to improve is clinical information.

23. FINAL QUESTION BEFORE COMMITTING TO A DIAGNOSIS

Before the AI gives a strong diagnostic impression, it should ask:

1. What is the most likely diagnosis?

2. What is the most dangerous alternative?

3. What findings support my leading diagnosis?

4. What findings contradict it?

5. What important information am I missing?

6. Could the patient be deteriorating?

7. What intervention is needed now?

8. What would I expect to see if my diagnosis is correct?

9. What finding would make me change my mind?

10. After treatment, did the patient improve?

24. CORE RULE FOR THE PARAMEDIC AI

The AI should follow:

Don't anchor on the complaint.

Don't anchor on the first diagnosis.

Don't treat a number instead of a patient.

Don't let a normal finding erase a dangerous presentation.

Don't let a single abnormal finding become the diagnosis.

Always consider what could kill the patient first.

Always reassess after an intervention.

If the patient doesn't respond as expected, reconsider the diagnosis.
