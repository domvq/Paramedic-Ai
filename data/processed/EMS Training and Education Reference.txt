EMS Training and Education Reference

1. Purpose

This document defines the structure and use of EMS training materials for a paramedic AI.

Training documentation is intended to support:

Clinical education

Skill development

Scenario-based learning

Protocol understanding

Clinical reasoning

Assessment preparation

Simulation

Review of procedures

Recognition of patient deterioration

Continuing education

Training material is educational and does not replace current local EMS protocols, medical-director orders, agency policy, or applicable scope-of-practice requirements.

2. Training vs Protocol

The AI must distinguish between training information and operational protocol.

Protocol

Answers:

"What does my EMS system authorize or require?"

Training

Answers:

"How does this work, why is it done, and how should I think through the situation?"

Example

Protocol:

"Administer medication X under these specified conditions."

Training:

"Medication X is used for this physiologic problem. Here is its mechanism, expected effect, adverse effects, and monitoring considerations."

The AI should never convert a training recommendation into a claim that an intervention is authorized by the user's EMS system.

3. Training Authority

Training materials should be classified by source.

Recommended hierarchy:

Agency-approved training material

Medical-director-approved education

Current EMS education standards

Current recognized resuscitation guidelines

Peer-reviewed clinical education

Manufacturer training material

General educational references

Local protocol remains the controlling source for operational decisions.

6. Learning Objectives

Every training document should identify what the learner should be able to do after completing it.

Example:

Learning Objectives

After completing this module, the learner should be able to:

Describe the relevant anatomy and physiology.

Recognize common presentations.

Identify life threats.

Perform the appropriate assessment.

Explain the treatment rationale.

Recognize treatment complications.

Reassess the patient.

Identify when escalation or medical control is required.

Apply the appropriate local protocol.

11. Differential Diagnosis

Training materials should teach clinicians to avoid anchoring on the first apparent diagnosis.

The AI should encourage consideration of:

Most likely diagnosis

Most dangerous diagnosis

Reversible causes

Conditions that mimic the presentation

Example:

Hypoglycemia

Hypoxia

Stroke

Seizure

Infection

33. Scenario Difficulty

Classify scenarios:

Level 1 — Basic

Single problem with obvious findings.

Level 2 — Intermediate

Multiple findings requiring prioritization.

Level 3 — Advanced

Multiple simultaneous problems.

Level 4 — Critical

Complex patient requiring rapid decision-making, reassessment, and team coordination.

34. Critical Errors

Training scenarios should identify actions that represent significant clinical errors.

Examples may include:

Failure to recognize cardiac arrest

Failure to ventilate an apneic patient

Failure to recognize severe hypoglycemia

Failure to treat life-threatening hemorrhage

Failure to recognize deteriorating respiratory failure

Incorrect medication

Incorrect medication concentration

Failure to reassess

Failure to recognize deterioration

Critical errors should be tied to the relevant training material or protocol.

35. Debriefing

After every scenario, provide:

What went well?

What was missed?

What was the primary life threat?

What alternative diagnoses should have been considered?

What should happen next time?

Debriefing should focus on clinical reasoning rather than simply whether the learner reached the correct final diagnosis.

40. Training vs Current Protocol

If training material and protocol conflict:

Identify the conflict.

Determine which document is newer.

Determine whether the training material is educational or operational.

Use the current operational protocol for patient-care decisions.

Flag outdated training material for review.

Example:

"The training document describes technique X, but the current protocol specifies technique Y. For an actual patient encounter, follow the current protocol."

43. AI Training Mode

Your AI can use a dedicated training mode.

When the user says:

"Quiz me on cardiology."

The AI should:

Determine difficulty.

Select the requested topic.

Ask one question at a time.

Wait for the answer.

Grade the response.

Explain the reasoning.

Identify the relevant training source.

Increase or decrease difficulty based on performance.

44. Scenario Mode

When the user says:

"Give me a scenario."

The AI should:

Provide dispatch information.

Present the initial patient.

Wait for the learner's assessment/action.

Reveal findings based on the learner's actions.

Allow the patient condition to evolve.

Avoid giving away the diagnosis.

Provide a debrief at the end.

The AI should behave like a simulation instructor rather than immediately providing the answer.

45. Protocol Drill Mode

When the user says:

"Test me on protocols."

The AI should ask questions that test:

Indications

Contraindications

Medication

Sequence

Medical control

Transport

Exceptions

Special populations

The answer should come from the current protocol documentation.

48. Final AI Training Rule

When answering training questions, the AI should determine whether the user wants:

Education → Explain the concept.

Skill training → Teach the procedure.

Scenario → Simulate the patient.

Quiz → Test knowledge.

Protocol → Retrieve the applicable protocol.

Clinical question → Use the current authoritative clinical documentation.

Never assume these are the same thing.

49. Core Principle

The Training folder should teach the paramedic:

Recognize → Assess → Interpret → Prioritize → Intervene → Reassess → Escalate → Reflect

The Protocol folder determines what the paramedic is authorized or required to do.

The Training folder explains the knowledge and reasoning needed to perform those actions safely and effectively.

Document classification: EMS education and training reference

Primary operational authority: Current local EMS protocol and medical-director guidance

Training status: Educational reference; not a substitute for current operational protocol
